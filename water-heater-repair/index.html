A water heater leaking onto the garage floor or into a closet pan needs attention within minutes. Water heater repair in American Canyon, CA starts with one question: does the water come from the tank shell, a fitting or the relief valve? We answer the phone 24/7 and dispatch a licensed plumber, with same-day appointments when available. A loose connector is a short job, and a rusted-through tank is not a repair at all.

While you wait, three moves limit the damage no matter what is leaking. Close the cold supply to the heater, shut off its fuel or power, and clear anything valuable off the floor. The rest of this page explains what each kind of leak means, what to check when the water simply runs cold, and how gas, electric and hybrid models differ.

Active problem

Water is pooling around the heater right now

.

Standing water under a heater is both a property problem and a safety problem. The water may be scalding, the floor may be slick, and an electric unit sitting in a puddle is a shock risk. Work in the order below and stop at any step that feels unsafe.

A steady drip into a bucket buys you time, but a fast stream does not. If the pan is overflowing or water is moving toward a wall, treat it as an active flood and call right away.

  1. Smell gas at any point? Leave the house first and do not touch light switches, lighters or anything that could spark. Call 911 from outside, then PG&E, the gas utility for most of the North Bay, at 1-800-743-5000..
  2. Shut off the fuel or power. On a gas heater, turn the control knob to off. On an electric one, switch off its breaker only if you can reach the panel without stepping through water..
  3. Close the cold water supply valve on the pipe entering the top of the heater. Turn the handle until it stops, or a quarter turn for a lever..
  4. Stay clear of the pipe that runs from the relief valve down toward the floor. Water from it can be hot enough to scald..
  5. Move boxes, tools and anything stored on the floor out of the water, and photograph the damage before cleanup for your records..
  6. Call (855) 575-2890 and tell the dispatcher the tank size if you know it, whether it is gas or electric, and exactly where the water is showing up..

Tank, fitting or relief valve: reading the puddle

.

Most callers describe the same scene: water on the floor, a heater that still seems to be working, and no idea where the water starts. The location of the wet spot narrows it down quickly. Water on top of the tank usually points to the pipe connections or the anode rod port, while water at the bottom could be almost anything that ran down the side.

Fittings are the best case. The flexible connectors, the union nuts and the shutoff valve above the heater all loosen or corrode over the years. Tightening or replacing one of them is a modest repair, and the heater keeps working afterward.

The relief valve is the second case. It is a safety device, and it opens when pressure or temperature climbs too high. A valve that drips once in a while may just be worn, but one that runs steadily often means pressure in the house plumbing is spiking, and swapping the valve alone will not cure that.

The tank shell is the bad case. If water seeps from the seam, from the jacket at the bottom or from under the insulation, the steel liner has failed. That heater is finished, and the honest next step is a conversation about replacing the water heater rather than paying for a repair that cannot hold.

No hot water, or hot water that quits halfway

.

A heater that has simply gone cold is a different call from a leak, and often a cheaper one. The cause depends on the fuel, and some of it you can check safely before dialing.

Do not remove covers or panels from a gas unit, and do not relight a pilot if you have smelled gas. For anything beyond the checks below, call and we will take it from there.

  • Electric: look for a tripped breaker, which can point to a failed heating element or thermostat..
  • Gas: check whether the control shows a fault light or code, and write down what it says for the dispatcher..
  • Water runs warm, then cold within minutes: a failed element, a broken dip tube or heavy sediment in the tank..
  • Water is hot but weak at every tap: that points to the house plumbing rather than the heater..
  • Water is scalding hot: the thermostat or gas valve may be failing, so turn the setting down and call..

Why a rusty tank cannot be patched

.

Inside every steel tank is a glass-like liner, and a sacrificial anode rod that corrodes first so the tank does not. Once the rod is used up, the steel begins to rust from the inside, slowly at first. Weeping at the seam and brown streaks down the jacket are the late signs.

If the tank is still sound, replacing a spent anode rod can extend its life, and we will say so honestly. If the shell has already rusted through, nothing from outside the tank will seal it. At that point it makes more sense to plan a new unit's installation than to keep paying for repairs.

Gas, electric and hybrid water heater repair compared

.

Gas heaters add the burner, the pilot or igniter, the thermocouple and the gas control valve to the list of things that fail. They also add a flue, which has to draw properly to carry exhaust out. Anything involving gas parts or venting is licensed work, and a smell of gas always overrides every other step on this page.

Electric heaters are simpler. Two heating elements and two thermostats do most of the work, and most no-hot-water calls come down to one of those parts. They have no flame, but they do need the power off before anyone opens a panel.

A tankless unit is a different animal, covered on the tankless water heater page. Hybrid or heat pump models combine an electric element with a compressor that pulls heat from the surrounding air. They have more parts, a condensate drain and an air filter that needs attention. If you own one, give the dispatcher the model number when you call so the right technician is sent.

What shapes the price of a water heater repair

.

Two heater repairs on the same street can cost very different amounts, and these are the reasons.

Which part failed

A connector or relief valve is a small job. An element, thermostat or gas control valve takes more time and a more expensive part.

Fuel type

Gas work involves venting and gas parts, and hybrid units involve compressor-side components, so each adds its own labor.

Access

A heater in an open garage is quick to reach. One tucked into a tight closet or an attic platform takes longer.

Age and condition

Corroded shutoffs and fittings can break when touched, which may turn one repair into several.

Time of the call

Late-night and weekend dispatch is priced differently from a planned daytime visit, and you hear the number first.

Many homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a tank that bursts, but not a slow leak or a worn-out heater itself. Policies differ, so ask your insurer what applies, and photograph the damage before any cleanup begins.
