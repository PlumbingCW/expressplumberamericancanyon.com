Emergency Faucet Repair in American Canyon, CA

.

Drips, stuck handles, leaking shower valves and shutoff valves that will not close are all repairable, and most start with the same two minutes.

(855) 575-2890

A faucet repair in American Canyon, CA covers the visible fixture and the valves behind it. That includes kitchen and bathroom faucets, tub and shower valves, the stop valves under the sink and the hose spigot on the side of the house.

The common thread is a worn seal, cartridge or stem that lets water through when it should not. Water that cannot be stopped is an emergency, and our 24/7 emergency dispatch sends a licensed and insured plumber.

If the fixture is dripping but controllable, you have time to gather details. If a valve will not turn off or water is spraying, jump to the urgent steps first. The sections after that explain what each type of fixture failure looks like and what happens at the repair.

Active problem

Water will not stop at the faucet, the valve or the wall

.

A broken stop valve under a sink, a handle that snapped off in your hand and a shower stem that sprays behind the wall all share one fix. Cut the supply upstream of the fixture, then deal with the part.

  1. Close the small oval or round stop valve under the sink, clockwise, until it stops. Use gentle force and no tools, since old valves can crack..
  2. If the stop valve is missing, stuck or leaking itself, go to the main water shutoff for the house and close it fully..
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house to let the pipes drain and relieve pressure on the broken fixture..
  4. Move anything valuable out of the cabinet, set a bucket under the leak and turn off power to any outlet that is wet..
  5. Take a photograph, then call (855) 575-2890 and tell us which valve or handle failed and whether the main shutoff held..

A shutoff valve that will not shut off

.

This is the most stressful version of a fixture problem, because the one device meant to stop water has failed. The cause is usually age. Stems corrode, washers harden, and valves that have not been turned in years seize half-open or leak around the handle once they move.

If the stop valve under a fixture does not hold, the next valve upstream is the main shutoff, normally near where the water line enters the house. If that one also fails, call the city's leak line listed in the local section below and ask what it can do at the meter. Do not force anything, and do not use a wrench on a valve that is already leaking.

  • Handle spins freely but water keeps flowing, so the stem inside is stripped.
  • Valve drips around the handle, so the packing is worn.
  • Valve closes but a thin stream still passes, so the seat is damaged.
  • Valve will not move at all, so scale or corrosion has locked it.

.

Most fixture calls fall into one of these patterns.

Water at the base of the handle.Loose packing or O-ring.Replace seals, clean the stem.
Showerhead drips after shutoff.Valve cartridge or diverter wearing.Replace the cartridge behind the handle.

Replacement is often better than repair when parts are no longer made for an older faucet.

Cartridges, washers and why a drip becomes a leak

.

Most modern faucets use a cartridge or a ceramic disc to control flow. A drip means the seal inside has worn, and the sealing surface often has tiny grooves. Tightening the handle will not fix that. The cartridge needs to come out and be matched exactly, since a nearly right part will leak again.

Single-handle kitchen faucets, two-handle bathroom faucets and tub valves all use different parts. We often take a quick look at the faucet model, or a photo of the tag, before arriving, so the correct cartridge is on hand. If you have it, mention the brand and where the handle sits.

Shower and tub valves leak into places you cannot see

.

A showerhead that drips for a minute after you shut it off is irritating. A leak at the valve behind the wall is worse, because the water goes into the framing, not into the tub. Stains on the ceiling below, a musty smell near the bathroom or peeling paint around the access panel are signs the valve or its connections are leaking.

In those cases the plumber replaces the cartridge or valve body and then checks whether water has traveled. If the source is unclear, a leak-finding visit under leak detection separates a fixture leak from a pipe leak. The answer affects whether the fix is a small repair or a wall opening.

What a faucet or valve repair involves

.
1

Stop the water

.

The plumber closes the stop valve or the main shutoff and opens the fixture to relieve pressure.

2

Identify the part

.

The fixture is taken apart, the failed seal, cartridge or stem is compared with the replacement, and nearby valves are tested.

3

Replace and reassemble

.

New parts go in, connections are tightened and the handle is aligned.

4

Pressure test

.

The water comes back slowly while we check for drips under the sink, behind the handle and at supply lines.

What drives the price of a faucet or valve repair

.

A cartridge swap and a wall valve replacement are different scale jobs, so costs vary with what is found.

Repair or replace the fixture

Parts are cheaper than a new faucet, but an older model with scarce parts can cost more to keep alive than to replace.

Access

A faucet on an open sink is quick. A tub or shower valve behind a tile wall may need a panel or tile opened.

Valve condition

A stop valve that fails can add replacement time, and a main valve that will not hold changes the scope of the visit.

Fixture quality

Brand, finish and style change the price of parts or a new faucet. Specialty finishes are often special order.

Hidden damage

Water in a wall or under a floor may need drying and repair beyond the plumbing.

Policies differ. Many cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a valve that fails and floods a room, but not a slow drip or a worn-out part. Photograph the damage before cleanup and ask your insurer what applies.
