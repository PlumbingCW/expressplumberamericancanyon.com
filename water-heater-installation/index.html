Most people meet water heater installation in a hurry. You may be planning ahead instead. Maybe a new build needs one, a remodel is moving the closet, or you want to swap an aging tank before it fails.

Water heater installation in American Canyon, CA means selecting a unit, connecting water and fuel or power, venting, bracing and testing it. Our line is staffed 24/7, but planned installs are best booked at a time that suits you.

A good install starts well before the plumber arrives, with decisions about size, fuel and location. This page walks through those choices, the basics of venting and gas supply, and the code points that homeowners should know exist.

Decide the size before the brand

.

Capacity is the choice people most often get wrong. A tank that is too small runs out when two showers overlap, and a tank that is too large keeps extra water warm all day for nothing. Think about how many people live in the house, how many bathrooms are in use at the same time and whether you run a dishwasher and washer in the morning.

Recovery rate matters as much as tank volume. It describes how fast the heater reheats once the hot water runs low, and it differs between gas and electric models. A gas unit generally recovers faster than an electric tank of the same size.

Physical space also limits the choice. Measure the height and width of the closet or garage corner, the clearance to walls and the path from the front door. A taller, narrower tank sometimes fits where a squat one does not.

Fuel choices and what each asks of your house

.

The fuel you choose decides what has to exist behind the wall before a heater can go in. Changing fuel is possible but adds lines, circuits or venting, while keeping the same fuel is the simpler path.

  • Gas: needs a gas supply line, a shutoff valve at the unit, combustion air and an exhaust vent that rises and terminates correctly..
  • Electric: needs a dedicated circuit of the right size and no venting, which makes placement more flexible..
  • Heat pump (hybrid): needs an electric circuit, open air space around it, a condensate drain and room for service access. We make no claims about rebates, so check with your electric provider..
  • Tankless: a different system with its own gas, venting and sizing rules, covered separately on the tankless water heater page..

Venting and gas line basics every owner should recognize

.

A gas water heater burns fuel, and the exhaust has to leave the building. The flue must slope, seal at its joints and end at an approved termination. A poor flue can let carbon monoxide into the house, which is why this is work for a licensed plumber and not a weekend project.

The gas line is the other half. Its size has to match the heater's input, the shutoff valve should be within reach, and every joint is tested for leaks before the unit is used. If a plumber proposes to change the line size or route, ask them to explain why.

Combustion air matters too. A heater sealed inside a tight closet without a way to draw air can burn poorly. If you plan to enclose the heater during a remodel, mention it when you call so the plumber can plan the air supply.

If you ever smell gas near any appliance, leave the building, call 911 and then call PG&E, the gas utility for most of the North Bay, at 1-800-743-5000.

Expansion tanks and relief valves in plain language

.

When water heats, it expands. In a closed plumbing system that has nowhere to go, pressure rises. A thermal expansion tank is a small tank with an air cushion that absorbs the extra volume, and some installations call for one.

Every tank heater also needs a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe that runs safely downward. This is the safety device that opens if things go wrong. Whether your home needs an expansion tank depends on the plumbing setup, and the plumber will check your pressure to see.

How the strapping law shapes a proper install

.

California does not leave a water heater free-standing. Health and Safety Code section 19211 says new and replacement heaters, along with existing residential ones, must be braced, anchored or strapped according to the California Plumbing Code, and preengineered kits exist for tanks up to 120 gallons.

In practice, that means the wall or framing behind the unit needs to be able to hold the straps, so tell the plumber about any remodeled or unusual walls ahead of time. The newest edition, the 2025 California Plumbing Code, took effect on January 1, 2026. An installer who skips bracing has not finished the job, so check that it is on your written price.

What affects the price of an installation

.

A planned install has the most room for choice, which is also why prices vary widely.

Unit type and size

Standard tank, higher-efficiency tank, heat pump and tankless models sit at very different price points.

Fuel changes

Moving from one fuel to another requires new gas lines, circuits or venting, which adds labor and materials.

Location and access

Moving the heater, or installing in a tight space, takes more time than putting it in the same spot.

Code parts

Bracing, relief valve piping, an expansion tank and proper shutoff valves are part of a correct install.

Permit and inspection

If your job requires a permit, there will be fees and scheduling to account for.

Insurance does not usually pay for a planned upgrade or for replacing a heater that simply wore out. It may respond to sudden water damage if a tank fails during or after an installation, so ask your insurer how your policy works and photograph the work.
