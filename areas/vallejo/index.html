A stain with no drip is still a leak

.

The most common Vallejo call that surprises people is the quiet one. A ceiling in a two-story house darkens, a baseboard softens, or the water bill climbs with no running fixture in sight. Water travels along framing and plaster before it shows itself, so the stain is rarely under the pipe that is failing.

Older homes make this harder, because walls were built to be repaired from the surface rather than opened from behind. Locating the leak first keeps the demolition small. In ZIP 94591, home to 56,305 of Vallejo's residents, leak detection is the call we expect most.

Until we arrive, shut the main valve if the stain is growing, and put a bucket under any slow drip. Do not poke at wet plaster or ceiling board, since a saturated ceiling can come down all at once.

Repiping is a decision, not a reflex

.

When pipes in a Victorian or Craftsman start leaking, someone always suggests replacing everything. Sometimes that is right, and sometimes a single failed section is the whole story. We do not assume what runs inside your walls from the neighborhood, because two houses on the same street can differ completely.

What we look at is the pattern. One leak after decades is a repair. Several leaks in a short span, rust-colored water, or low pressure at every fixture suggest tired pipe, and then a whole-house repipe deserves a real conversation with a clear price first.

Vallejo's two water systems, and what to ask about yours

.

The City of Vallejo runs two separate systems. The Vallejo Water System draws from Lake Berryessa through the Putah South Canal and from the State Water Project through the North Bay Aqueduct, blended at Fleming Hill. The Lakes Water System draws from Lake Berryessa and from Lakes Frey and Madigan, treated at the Green Valley Water Treatment Plant.

Both use chlorine, and Vallejo reports lead was not detected in 90 percent of homes sampled. We state no hardness number for Vallejo, because the real figure belongs to the utility. Ask Vallejo Water at (707) 649-3472 for the hardness in its annual report, then decide whether a softener or filter is worth pricing.

When the sewer line is the problem

.

A backup is the one emergency where waiting makes the mess bigger. If several drains gurgle at once, the trouble is probably past the last branch, in the line leaving the house. Stop running water, keep everyone away from the wet area, and call us.

Repairs on a public sewer, side sewer or building lateral can require a permit, and who owns which stretch of line depends on your city, so ask Vallejo before anyone digs. We can describe what a camera shows, and you take that to the city.

  • Stop using sinks, tubs and the washing machine.
  • Keep children and pets out of the wet area.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

.

In a Vallejo house with old walls, ten minutes of the right moves can keep a repair from becoming a rebuild.

1

Shut off the main valve

.

Find the main water shutoff, often at the front of the house or near the meter box, and turn it fully off. If it will not turn, leave it and say so on the phone.

2

Kill power to wet areas

.

If water is near outlets, a ceiling light or an appliance, switch off that breaker at the panel. Stay out of standing water.

3

Clear the area

.

Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the wet floor, and open a path to the valve and the damaged spot so work can start immediately.

4

Call the 24/7 line

.

Dial (855) 575-2890 at any hour. Tell us your street, your ZIP, and whether the house is older, so a licensed plumber arrives with the right parts.
