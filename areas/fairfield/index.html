162 ppm: what Fairfield's water does to heaters and fixtures

.

The City of Fairfield reports hardness between 72 and 229 ppm, with an average of 162 ppm. That is about 9.5 grains per gallon, noticeably harder than the 92 ppm average in American Canyon. Dissolved minerals come out of hot water as scale, which collects on tank bottoms, heating elements and the inside of faucets.

A tank heater in hard water works harder to heat the same water, because scale sits between the burner or element and the water. Over time that means noise, slower recovery and a shorter life. When a heater finally fails, water heater repair is the first question, and replacement the second.

What Fairfield homes need, in the order the water suggests

.

Tank heaters that fight scale

.

Noisy tanks, rusty-looking hot water and slow recovery are common signs of mineral buildup. A repair visit shows whether a part can be saved or the tank has reached the end.

Tankless units that need descaling

.

A tankless heater has narrow passages that scale clogs fast in water this hard. Tankless descaling and service keeps one running, and sizing matters before a swap.

Water treatment

.

Where hardness runs high, a softener or filter becomes a serious option. We lay out what water softener service involves and what it does not fix.

A city that went from 14,968 to 44,146 in ten years

.

Fairfield counted 3,118 people in 1950, 14,968 in 1960 and 44,146 in 1970. By 2000 it had 96,178, and the 2020 count was 119,881. So a large share of the city's housing dates from the 1960s onward, and many homes have decades of use behind their water lines and fixtures.

We do not guess what any house is plumbed with from its decade. Age is a reason to watch for repeated leaks, worn shutoff valves and failing faucet cartridges, and faucet and fixture repair is often the cheapest place to start. Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

.

A hot-water leak can do damage fast, and a heater that is still heating makes it worse.

1

Shut off the main valve

.

Turn off the main shutoff first. If the leak is from the water heater, also close the cold-water valve on top of the tank, and do not open any hot taps.

2

Kill power to wet areas

.

Switch off the breaker for an electric heater. For a gas heater, turn the gas control to off, and leave the area at once if you smell gas.

3

Clear the area

.

Move storage, boxes and tools away from the heater and the wet floor. Open a path so the plumber can reach the tank and the valves.

4

Call the 24/7 line

.

Call (855) 575-2890 at any hour. Tell us if the unit is a tank or tankless, gas or electric, and what you hear or see.

If you smell gas near the heater

.

Gas smell near a water heater is an emergency of its own. Leave the home, call 911 from outside, then call PG&E, the gas utility for most of the North Bay, at 1-800-743-5000. Do not use matches, flashlights or anything that could spark.

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 also requires residential water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion. If a replacement is coming, ask us to include the bracing in the price.
