Napa has a particular relationship with water. The rainy season runs October to April, the river has flooded downtown within living memory, and the city's water arrives from three different sources. Each of those facts changes what a Napa homeowner should do before, during and after a plumbing emergency.

October to April is when Napa drains get tested

.

Napa averages 31.10 inches of rain a year (1991 to 2020), and most of it falls in a seven-month rainy season. Saturated ground and heavy runoff load every pipe leaving a house, and slow drains that behaved all summer start to complain. That is the season when drain cleaning calls climb.

A drain that backs up only during storms is telling you something specific. Either the line leaving the house is partly blocked and cannot keep up, or water is getting into the line from outside. Clearing it before the next storm is cheaper than cleaning up after it, so do not wait for a dry week to call.

What the 1986 and 2005 floods mean for your plumbing

.

The Napa River flooded in 1986 and again in 2005, when it reached downtown. Voters funded the Napa River Flood Project with a sales tax in March 1998, and work covered about 6 miles of the river. We are plumbers, not flood engineers, so we make no claim about how much protection any single address has.

What matters for your plumbing is the practical side. In a flood-prone area, know where your main shutoff valve is before the storm, keep the path to it clear, and ask a plumber whether a backflow preventer suits your lot. Homes inside a flood zone should also keep valuables off lower floors during the rainy season.

Three water sources, one hardness number worth knowing

.

The City of Napa Water Division draws from Lake Hennessey, Lake Milliken, and Barker Slough through the North Bay Aqueduct. Which plant runs depends on conditions, so the water at your tap can change through the year. The 2024 report puts total hardness at an average of 114 ppm, ranging from 68 to 179 ppm, which is about 6.7 grains per gallon.

That is moderate. For a water heater, it means scale builds slowly but does build, so an annual flush is cheap insurance. If you are weighing a water softener, test your tap first, since the figure varies from month to month. The report also says there are no public-system lead service lines.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

.

In Napa's wet season, the water may be coming from inside and outside at once, so sort out which first.

1

Shut off the main valve

.

Find the shutoff before any storm, then turn it fully off if a pipe has burst. The City of Napa Water Division emergency line, (707) 253-4451, handles problems on the street side.

2

Kill power to wet areas

.

If water reaches an outlet, a garage appliance or a lower-floor circuit, switch off that breaker. Do not wade into standing water near electricity.

3

Clear the area

.

Lift belongings off floors that are taking on water, and move vehicles or equipment away from the damaged spot. Clear the path to the valve.

4

Call the 24/7 line

.

Call (855) 575-2890 any hour, including stormy weekends. Tell us whether water is coming through pipes, drains or both, and a licensed plumber is dispatched.

Gas, heaters and storm outages

.

A storm outage can leave a gas water heater with a pilot that went out, and relighting is not a guess-and-check job. If you smell gas, leave the home and call 911, then PG&E, the gas utility for most of the North Bay, at 1-800-743-5000. Do not use matches, flashlights or anything that could spark.

California also requires residential water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion, under Health and Safety Code section 19211. If yours leans after a shake or a flood, call before relighting it.
