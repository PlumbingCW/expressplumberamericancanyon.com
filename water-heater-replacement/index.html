How to tell the tank itself has failed

.

Some failures are the tank, and some are parts attached to it. The difference decides whether you spend a little or replace the whole unit. Water coming from the shell, a seam or the bottom edge is the clearest sign that the steel liner has given up.

Rusty hot water, a rumbling noise from sediment baking at the bottom, and a pan that keeps refilling are the other late signs. A heater that has needed several repairs in a short stretch is also telling you something. Parts keep wearing out in a tank whose core is already tired.

Check the data plate on the side of the heater. It lists the capacity and usually a manufacture date in the serial number. If you can read it to the dispatcher, the conversation about repair or replace gets much shorter.

Earthquake bracing is a legal requirement, so read your invoice for it

.

Under California Health and Safety Code section 19211, a new or replacement water heater has to be braced, anchored or strapped to resist shaking, following the California Plumbing Code. The rule also covers existing residential heaters. For standard tanks up to 120 gallons, preengineered strapping kits are available, so the work is not exotic.

The 2025 California Plumbing Code took effect on January 1, 2026, and the 2022 edition took effect on January 1, 2023. A replacement done today falls under the current edition. For you, that means the bracing hardware and the labor to install it should be named on the written price, not added at the end. Ask for it to be listed, and ask the plumber to show you the strapping when the job is done.

What the replacement visit includes, in order

.
1

Shut off and drain

.

Water supply and fuel or power are isolated, and the old tank is drained so it can be disconnected.

2

Remove the old unit

.

Connections, venting and any old bracing come off, and the pan and surrounding area are checked for damage.

3

Set and connect the new tank

.

The new heater is positioned, plumbed in with new connectors, and wired or gas-connected as required.

4

Vent, relief valve and bracing

.

Exhaust venting, the temperature and pressure relief valve with its discharge pipe, and earthquake strapping are all put in place.

5

Fill, test and walk through

.

The tank is filled and purged, brought to temperature and checked for leaks, then you get a quick tour of the shutoff valve and controls.

Heat pump water heaters, honestly

.

A heat pump water heater moves heat from the surrounding air into the tank, so it uses less electricity than a standard electric element. It needs open air space around it, a condensate drain and a suitable electrical circuit, and it makes some noise.

Whether it is the right swap depends on the room, the existing wiring and your budget, and any savings depend on your electric rates. We do not promise any rebate or incentive, so check with your electric provider and the state before counting on one.

Why the 2014 earthquake still matters at the strap

.

The August 24, 2014 South Napa earthquake had its epicenter about 3.7 miles northwest of American Canyon. A full tank weighs hundreds of pounds, and shaking can tip an unbraced heater, rupturing gas and water lines at the same time. That is why the strapping on your new unit is not a box-ticking exercise.

When you review your price, look for the bracing line and ask how the unit is anchored. If you are replacing a heater in a garage or closet, tell the plumber what is on the wall behind it, so the strap points can go into solid framing. Homes in Napa and other nearby towns face the same regional shaking, and we serve American Canyon and nearby towns.

What decides the cost of a replacement

.

Replacement prices vary a lot, and the reasons are specific to your house and the unit you choose.

Capacity and type

A larger tank, a higher-efficiency model or a heat pump unit costs more than a basic standard tank.

Fuel and venting

Switching between gas and electric means new lines, circuits or venting, and a like-for-like swap is simpler.

Code items

Bracing, a relief valve and discharge pipe, and sometimes an expansion tank are part of doing the job to current rules.

Existing condition

Corroded shutoff valves, old connectors or a damaged pan can add work once the old heater is out.

Timing and access

An emergency swap in a tight closet differs from a scheduled job in an open garage.

Insurance often responds to sudden damage from a burst tank, such as soaked flooring, but rarely pays for the worn-out heater itself. Policies differ, so ask your insurer before work begins, and photograph everything before cleanup.
