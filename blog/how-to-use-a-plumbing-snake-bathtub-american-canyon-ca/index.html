How to use a plumbing snake in a bathtub drain

.

By the American Canyon Express Plumbing team · Published July 25, 2026

Step-by-step help for snaking a clogged bathtub drain in American Canyon, CA: what to buy, how to clear hair, and when to stop.

(855) 575-2890

Standing in ankle-deep gray water is a good reason to learn how to use a plumbing snake. A hand-crank drain snake can clear a hair clog in a bathtub drain in about twenty minutes, and it costs far less than a service call if the blockage is close to the opening.

The steps below cover the tool, the setup, the motion that actually works, and the point where you should put the snake down. Read the safety section first. A snake is the wrong tool if a chemical drain cleaner is sitting in the tub.

Check these three things before you touch the snake

.

A short pause here prevents most accidents and most wasted effort.

  • Did anyone pour a chemical cleaner into this drain in the last day or two?.
  • Is the water draining slowly, or is it not moving at all?.
  • Is the toilet or a sink nearby also gurgling or backing up?.

Do not snake a drain that has chemical cleaner standing in it

.

Caustic and acid drain cleaners are strong enough to burn skin and eyes. When the cleaner sits in the trap, a snake stirs and splashes it back up at you. The fumes are also unpleasant in a small bathroom.

If you poured something in and it did not work, do not add a second product and do not start cranking. Leave the water alone, ventilate the room, keep kids and pets out and call us at (855) 575-2890. Tell the plumber exactly which product went in so the right gloves and eye protection come along.

What you need for a tub drain

.

Buy a hand-crank drum auger with a cable about one quarter inch thick and 15 feet long. It is sold at hardware stores and is easier to control than a long, thin plastic strip. Skip the power drill attachments, because a cable that spins too fast can whip and tear a hole in the pipe.

Gather rubber gloves, safety glasses, a screwdriver, old towels, a trash bag, a flashlight and a bucket. An old toothbrush helps with the final cleanup of the strainer and stopper.

Snaking the drain, one step at a time

.
1

Remove the stopper or strainer

.

Unscrew the strainer or lift out the pop-up stopper. Wipe off the hair wrapped around it and drop it in the trash bag.

2

Look down the hole

.

Shine the flashlight and pull out anything you can see by hand. Often the clog is a wad of hair sitting an inch or two below the opening.

3

Feed the cable gently

.

Push the tip of the snake into the drain and turn the handle clockwise. Let the cable do the work and advance a few inches at a time.

4

Stop when you feel resistance

.

That is usually the clog or the bend of the trap. Turn the crank in the same direction and push lightly. Do not force it.

5

Pull the cable back slowly

.

Draw it out while still turning, with a towel in your hand to catch whatever comes up. Wipe the cable as you go.

6

Flush with warm water

.

Run the tap for a few minutes. If the tub drains with a smooth swirl, repeat the whole process once more to be sure.

Why hair clogs form in a tub and how the snake handles them

.

Hair does not dissolve. It rides down the drain on soap and body oil, catches on a rough spot or the crossbar of the strainer, and builds a mat that grows a little every shower. Soap film turns that mat into a rubbery plug.

A snake works because the tip hooks or chops the mat and either drags it out or breaks it into pieces small enough to wash through. Expect a gray, smelly clump on the cable. That is normal and a good sign.

Once the drain is clear, a cheap hair catcher over the opening is the best long-term fix. Empty it after each shower and the clog will not return as often.

What you feel on the cable and what to do

.

The feel of the snake tells you a lot about what is in the pipe.

Cable stops hard at a bend.The trap or an elbow.Back off slightly, turn and try again with light pressure.

When to stop and call

.

A snake is meant for blockages that sit close to the tub. If the cable runs out before you meet resistance, or if clearing the tub makes the sink or toilet nearby gurgle, the clog is deeper in the drain line. A homeowner tool cannot reach it safely.

This is when professional drain cleaning makes sense. A plumber uses a longer machine with different cutting heads and can work from a cleanout. If the blockage is heavy grease or packed debris, hydro jetting may be the better method because it washes the pipe wall clean instead of poking a hole through the plug.

Put the snake down if any of this happens

.

Do not keep pushing against a problem you cannot see.

  • The cable kinks or knots in your hand.
  • Water backs up into another fixture.
  • You see rust-colored water or flakes of pipe.
  • You feel the pipe move or hear it crack.
  • The drain still will not clear after two careful passes.

How the cost of a tub drain call varies

.

A hand-crank snake costs very little, but when you call a plumber the price depends on a few things.

How deep the clog is

A blockage near the tub clears faster than one in the main line, and the machine and time needed change.

Access to the drain

A tub with a clean opening is simpler than one that needs an overflow plate or panel removed.

Whether other fixtures are affected

A single slow tub is usually one job. Several slow fixtures point at the branch or main line.

Method used

A cable machine and hydro jetting are different tools, and they are priced differently.

Homeowner policies differ. Many cover sudden and accidental water damage but not a slow drain or worn pipes. If a backup damaged flooring or a ceiling, photograph it before cleanup and ask your insurer what applies.

American Canyon note: where the rinse water goes

.

After snaking, you will have a bucket of gray water and a cable covered in sludge. Do not dump either into the street or a storm inlet. The City of American Canyon says only rain belongs in the storm drain, because storm drains flow untreated to local creeks, then the Napa River and San Francisco Bay.

Rinse the cable over the tub or a utility sink and let the water go down the sanitary drain, then bag the hair and sludge for the trash. If you have questions about stormwater rules, Environmental Services is at (707) 647-5325.

The same drain tips work in Vallejo, Napa and Fairfield

.

Most bathtub clogs are the same wherever you live. We take calls from Vallejo, Napa and Fairfield about hair and soap mats, and the snake method above is the same in each town.

What changes is how far the tub sits from the main line, which depends on the house and not on the city.
