How to Repair a Leaky Outdoor Faucet Handle in American Canyon, CA

.

By the American Canyon Express Plumbing team · Published September 13, 2026

Repair a leaky outdoor faucet handle in American Canyon, CA: tighten the packing nut, swap the washer, and know when to call (855) 575-2890 for the spigot.

(855) 575-2890

Water seeps out around the handle every time you turn the hose on, and the spigot drips onto the siding or the patio. In most cases the fix is small. A loose packing nut, a worn packing washer or a tired rubber washer inside the stem is the usual culprit, and you can often deal with it in under an hour with a wrench and a screwdriver.

This guide covers the order that works. Shut the water off first, tighten before you replace, replace before you give up. It also marks the point where a leak is no longer a handle problem and a licensed plumber should take over. For anything past a worn washer, faucet repair is the right call at (855) 575-2890.

Where the water is actually coming from

.

Outdoor faucets leak in three places, and each one points to a different part. Water around the handle stem means the packing is worn or the nut is loose. A steady drip from the spout when the handle is off means the washer inside has failed. Water leaking at the wall, behind the faucet body, means the problem is in the pipe or the connection and not the handle.

Run the hose and watch for a minute. Then turn the handle all the way off, wipe the faucet dry, and see which spot gets wet again. Ten minutes of looking saves you from replacing parts that were never broken.

Fix a leak at the handle in six moves

.
1

Shut the water off first

.

Close the interior shut-off valve for that line if you have one. If not, close the main, then open the faucet to drain what is left in the pipe.

2

Try the packing nut

.

The nut sits just behind the handle. Tighten it about a quarter turn with an adjustable wrench, turn the water back on, and check. Overtightening makes the handle hard to turn and can crack old brass.

3

Remove the handle

.

Back out the screw in the center and pull the handle off. If it is stuck, wiggle it gently instead of prying hard.

4

Unscrew the packing nut and look

.

Packing is the graphite string or small rubber ring wrapped around the stem. If it is crumbly or flat, that is your leak.

5

Replace the packing or washer

.

Take the old part to the hardware store so the size matches. Seat the new packing, thread the nut back on snugly, and reassemble.

6

Turn the water on slowly and test

.

Open the handle partly, then fully, then close it. Check the stem, the spout and the wall.

What you need before you start

.

Gather the parts first so the water is off for as short a time as possible.

  • An adjustable wrench and a screwdriver that fits the handle screw.
  • A replacement packing washer or packing string, plus a stem washer in the right size.
  • A small bucket and a rag for the water left in the line.
  • Plumber's grease for the stem threads.
  • Safety glasses if the old parts are corroded and breaking apart.

If the spout drips after the handle is shut

.

A drip from the spout with the handle fully closed is a worn seat washer at the end of the stem. The rubber has flattened or cracked, so it no longer seals against the seat.

With the water off, pull the stem out after removing the packing nut. The washer sits on the end, held by a small screw. Swap in a matching washer and tighten the screw. If the seat inside the faucet body is pitted, the new washer will only buy a few weeks, and the whole faucet should be replaced.

Keep the hose off the faucet while you work. A hose left attached can hold pressure against the spout and mask what the faucet is doing.

Stop and call a plumber if you see any of these

.

Some leaks are warnings that the problem is behind the wall, and tightening anything will not help. Call for leak detection when water appears at the base of the faucet where it meets the house, when a wet patch shows up inside along that wall, or when the stem will not move at all.

The same goes for a handle that spins freely, a stem that breaks off in your hand, or corroded threads that crumble. Forcing those parts risks a bigger flow of water than the drip you started with. If the line will not shut off, close the main valve and call (855) 575-2890 right away.

What to do about a faucet that is simply worn out

.

If the faucet is old, rusted, or has been repaired several times, replacing the whole spigot is often cheaper in the end than another set of washers. A plumber can also swap an ordinary spigot for a frost-free model or add a vacuum breaker that keeps hose water from flowing back into the house plumbing.

Replacement involves cutting and rejoining the pipe where it passes through the wall, which is where a small DIY job turns into a plumbing job. A bad joint inside the wall leaks quietly into the framing. That is a good reason to let a licensed plumber handle it.

American Canyon note: hardness and your washers

.

The City of American Canyon's 2023 water quality report lists average total hardness of 92 ppm, with a range of 60 to 188 ppm. That works out to about 5.4 grains per gallon, which is moderate.

For a faucet owner it means mineral crust on an old stem is possible but usually mild, so soaking and wiping the threads is often enough before reassembly. Test your tap or ask the water utility if you want your own figure.

Water in nearby cities differs. Fairfield, to the east, reports an average of 162 ppm, noticeably harder, so homes there may see heavier scale on stems and seats. If your address sits close to the city line, check which utility sends your bill, because a few American Canyon addresses are served by Vallejo's system.

What affects the cost of an outdoor faucet repair

.

A packing or washer swap is the lowest tier of work and usually needs little more than parts and a short visit. The price climbs when the faucet body must be replaced, when a shut-off valve is missing and must be added, or when the pipe behind the wall must be opened.

Access matters as well. A spigot on a flat wall behind a shrub is easy to work on. One tucked behind a fence or above a crawlspace opening takes more time. A clear price comes before any work starts, and the real number for your home comes from a call to (855) 575-2890.

A short list of habits that keep the faucet from leaking again

.

Small habits cut wear on the stem and washers.

  • Close the handle by hand until it stops, without cranking it.
  • Disconnect hoses when you are not using them.
  • Open the faucet slowly so the water does not hammer the stem.
  • Wipe and lightly grease the stem threads once a year.

Common questions answered

.

Water around the handle usually means the packing nut has loosened or the packing around the stem has worn out. Tightening the nut about a quarter turn fixes many of these leaks. If the leak continues, replace the packing washer. Do not tighten hard, because brass can crack and the handle can seize.

Close the interior shut-off valve for the outdoor line if you have one. If you do not, close the main water valve for the house, then open the faucet to drain the line. Do the work with the water off, and turn it back on slowly while watching the stem and the wall.

A packing nut tightening takes a few minutes. A packing or washer replacement is a short job once you have the right parts. Corroded or stuck parts take more time and care. If the stem will not budge, stop, because forcing it can break the stem and open the line.

Treat it as urgent when water flows steadily at the wall or into the house, when the faucet will not shut off, or when you see wet drywall inside along that wall. Shut off the main valve and call (855) 575-2890. We answer the phone 24/7 and dispatch a licensed plumber.

Mineral buildup can wear stems and seats over time, and it makes old parts harder to remove. American Canyon's 2023 report lists moderate hardness, an average of 92 ppm, so heavy scale is less common here than in harder-water towns. Wipe and grease the threads when you reassemble.
