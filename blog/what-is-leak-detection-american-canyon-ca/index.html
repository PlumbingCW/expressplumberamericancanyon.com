What Is Leak Detection and How Does It Work? A Guide for American Canyon Homeowners

.

By the American Canyon Express Plumbing team · Published August 24, 2026

Leak detection finds hidden water leaks without tearing out walls or floors.

(855) 575-2890

Leak detection is the use of listening gear, pressure tests, moisture sensing, and sometimes a thermal camera to find where water is escaping from a pipe you cannot see. The goal is to locate the leak within a small area so a repair opens up a patch of wall or floor, not a whole room. A plumber starts by confirming a leak exists, then narrows down where it is.

If your water bill climbed with no change in habits, or you hear water running when everything is off, this guide is for you. It explains the methods, a meter test you can do yourself, and when it is time for professional leak detection or, for a leak under concrete, slab leak repair.

The water bill went up and nobody changed anything

.

That is the most common way a hidden leak announces itself. The pipes look fine, the faucets do not drip, and yet usage keeps rising. Other hints include a warm patch on the floor, a musty smell in a cabinet, or the sound of a faint hiss at night.

Plumbers call the response to this situation leak detection. It is a method, not a single tool, and the right approach depends on whether the pipe is pressurized supply, a drain, or a line under a slab.

Try this before you call: the meter check

.

You can learn whether water is leaking with no tools beyond your eyes. Shut off every fixture inside and outside, including ice makers and irrigation. Then find your water meter and look at the small dial or low-flow indicator.

If it moves while nothing is running, water is going somewhere. If it holds still, the leak may be intermittent or only show when a fixture is on, and a plumber can test further.

  • Make sure no one flushes a toilet or starts a dishwasher during the test..
  • Take a photo of the meter, wait about an hour, and check again..
  • A moving dial with all fixtures off suggests a leak somewhere past the meter..
  • Do not pry open meter boxes. Only look through the lid, and call the utility if it is damaged..

How plumbers actually find the leak

.

Acoustic listening is the most familiar method. A sensitive microphone picks up the hiss or vibration a pressurized leak makes in the pipe. The plumber moves along the floor or wall and marks where the sound is strongest.

Pressure testing isolates sections of the system. The plumber closes off part of the pipes and watches whether pressure drops. A drop means the leak lies in that section, which cuts down the search.

Thermal imaging reads surface temperature. A hot water line leaking under a floor can warm the area above it, and the camera shows that pattern. It supports the other methods but does not replace them, because damp spots from other causes can look alike.

What a detection visit looks like

.
1

Talk through symptoms

.

The plumber asks about the bill, sounds, damp areas, and when you first noticed them.

2

Confirm the leak

.

A meter test or pressure test shows whether water is truly escaping and which side of the meter it is on.

3

Pinpoint the spot

.

Listening gear and moisture tools narrow the leak to a small area, which is marked.

4

Choose the repair

.

You get a clear price before work starts, and the plumber explains how much needs to be opened up.

What leak detection does not do

.

Detection finds a leak. It does not fix it. A separate repair follows, and a leak in a wall may mean drywall work afterward that a plumber does not handle.

It also cannot always find a leak the first time, particularly if it is tiny or only drips under certain pressure. We say so up front. If the first pass does not locate it, the next step is usually to test other sections, not to open walls at random.

Mold and health effects are outside what a plumber diagnoses. If you see mold or have health concerns, speak with a qualified specialist.

When it is under concrete

.

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe running below a concrete floor. Signs include a floor that feels warm, the sound of running water with fixtures off, and cracks that show moisture. Detection comes first, because breaking concrete in the wrong place is costly.

Neighbors in Vallejo and Napa call about the same symptoms, and the response is the same everywhere. Locate first, then choose between a spot repair, a reroute, or replacing the line.

Stop and call if you see any of these

.

Some situations are not for a meter check and a wait-and-see approach. Treat them as urgent.

If water is spraying, shut off the main valve at once. If a ceiling is bulging with water, keep clear of it and turn off power to that area at the breaker only if it is safe to reach. We dispatch a licensed plumber around the clock.

  • Water coming through a ceiling or light fixture..
  • Standing water around a gas appliance. Do not touch it..
  • A smell of gas, which means leave, call 911, then PG&E..
  • Bulging or sagging drywall..
