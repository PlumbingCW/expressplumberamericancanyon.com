Cleaning Out Storm Drains in American Canyon, CA: What You Can Safely Clear

.

By the American Canyon Express Plumbing team · Published September 8, 2026

Cleaning out storm drains in American Canyon, CA: what to sweep from your gutter, what to report to the city, and when a plumber at (855) 575-2890 helps.

(855) 575-2890

The gutter in front of your house is packed with leaves, and rain is coming. You can rake that debris off the street and put it in your yard waste bin. You should not push it down the grate. The storm drain at the curb belongs to the city, so a homeowner's job is limited to keeping the surface clear and keeping the wrong things out of it.

Trouble inside your own property is a different matter. A backed-up yard drain or a downspout line that will not carry water away is private plumbing, and drain cleaning is the right fix. This guide sorts out which problem you have, what is safe to do with a broom and gloves, and when to call (855) 575-2890.

Two different drains, two different owners

.

A storm drain carries rain from streets to local creeks. A sewer carries what goes down your sinks, tubs and toilets to the treatment plant. They are separate systems and they never share a pipe. Mixing them up is the most common reason people call the wrong place.

The grate at the curb and the pipe under the street are public. The drain in your side yard, the line that takes your downspouts away, and the cleanout on your sewer lateral are on your property. If water is pooling at the curb, the city is the right contact. If water is pooling beside your foundation, that is yours to sort out.

What you can safely clear by hand

.

Staying on the sidewalk and street edge in front of your own home, these are reasonable things to do on a dry day.

  • Rake or sweep leaves and litter off the gutter and bag them for yard waste.
  • Pick up trash caught against the grate with a gloved hand or a grabber tool.
  • Keep your own lawn clippings, soil and mulch from washing toward the curb.
  • Take photos of a grate that is broken, sunken or missing and note the nearest address.
  • Stop when you see anything that looks like oil, paint, chemicals or a strong sewage smell.

Not sure which case applies to your home? Call (855) 575-2890 and describe what you see.

Why the grate is not a trash can

.

The city is plain about it. Only rain belongs in the storm drain. Storm drains in American Canyon flow untreated to local creeks, then the Napa River and San Francisco Bay. Nothing is filtered between your gutter and the water.

That is why a pile of leaves is more than a nuisance. Leaves swept into a grate carry dirt and whatever is on the street with them. They also clog the inlet, and a clogged inlet floods the road in the next storm.

Soap from car washing, pet waste, paint rinse and oil from a leaking engine all follow the same path. A driveway wash that runs to the curb is a discharge to the creek. Wash the car on the lawn, where the soil takes it, or at a commercial car wash.

American Canyon note: the city's stormwater rules

.

The city says putting leaves in gutters or inlets violates Municipal Code 14.28. That sounds strict for something as ordinary as leaves, but it explains why the right move is to bag the debris, not hose it down the street. If you have a large tree, plan to sweep on a regular schedule so a pile never sits at the curb through a storm.

Public Works vacuums storm drain inlets before the wet season. That is helpful, but it does not cover the debris that falls afterward. Questions about what may go into a drain, or about a dumping problem, go to the city's Environmental Services line at (707) 647-5325. Ask the city before assuming a rule does or does not apply to your property.

When the problem is on your side of the property line

.

A yard drain grate that stays full of water after a rain usually means silt, roots or a crushed section of pipe somewhere downstream. Pulling out the leaves on top helps only if the pipe underneath is open. If the water still sits there an hour after the rain stops, the pipe is clogged or damaged.

A plumber can run a cable through the line and, when needed, use high-pressure water to clear packed silt. For a line that clears and then backs up again, a camera shows whether a joint has shifted. Our guide to drain cleaning explains how that work goes, and the same equipment handles outdoor lines.

Buildings with a lot of paved ground behave differently. A restaurant, an auto shop or a small retail center has private inlets that collect runoff and, often, wash water. Those lines get silt and grease buildup faster than a residential yard. Commercial plumbing service covers them, and the property owner should confirm with the city what the discharge rules are for that site.

Clearing a private yard drain grate, step by step

.
1

Wait for dry weather

.

Do not stand on wet, slick ground or work near a fast flow of water, and never reach into a pipe opening.

2

Lift the grate with a tool

.

Use a flat bar or the tool the grate was designed for, and keep your fingers clear of the edge so it cannot pinch.

3

Scoop out the debris by hand

.

Wear heavy gloves, bag leaves, silt and trash, and keep all of it out of the pipe.

4

Pour in a little clean water

.

A gentle flow tells you whether the pipe moves water. Slow or still water means a blockage deeper down, so stop there.

5

Replace the grate and call if it did not clear

.

A line that stays full needs a cable or a camera, and forcing a garden hose into it can push the clog further in.

Timing the work around the rainy season

.

Do your clearing in early autumn, before the first heavy storm. A neighbor town like Napa lists its rainy season as October to April, so plan your cleanup before then. A drain that is open in October has the best chance of staying open through the winter.

What shapes the cost of a private drain job

.

Price depends on how far the blockage sits from the opening, how many bends the pipe has, and whether the grate is easy to reach. Silt and roots take more passes than loose leaves. A line that needs a camera inspection before or after cleaning adds time.

Pipe condition matters too. Clay, cast iron and thin plastic pipe respond differently to cleaning, and a collapsed section turns a cleaning visit into a repair. You get a clear price before work starts, and the only way to learn it for your yard is a call to (855) 575-2890.

Homeowners across the area face the same questions, whether they live here or in Vallejo, about eight miles south. The yard and the pipe decide the cost, not the zip code.
