Water Heater Leaking From the Bottom: What to Shut Off and Who to Call in American Canyon, CA

.

By the American Canyon Express Plumbing team · Published September 23, 2026

Water heater leaking from the bottom in American Canyon, CA? Shut off power or gas and the cold inlet, find the source, and call (855) 575-2890 right away.

(855) 575-2890

A puddle is spreading around the base of the water heater. Is it a fixable drip or a dead tank? Start by making it safe. Shut off the power or the gas, close the cold water inlet on top of the tank, and then work out where the water is coming from. A leak from a valve or a fitting can often be repaired. Water seeping from the tank itself means the tank has failed.

Call early. A slow leak can soak a floor, wall framing and anything stored nearby, and a failing tank can release a lot of water at once. Call (855) 575-2890 for water heater repair if a valve is the culprit, or to plan a replacement if the tank has given out.

Do these things before you look for the cause

.

Water and electricity do not mix, and a gas heater carries its own risk. Safety comes before diagnosis, and the steps below take a couple of minutes.

  • Electric heater: switch off its breaker at the panel, without stepping into standing water to do it..
  • Gas heater: if you smell gas, leave the home and call 911, then PG&E, the gas utility for most of the North Bay, at 1-800-743-5000. Do not use matches, flashlights or anything that could spark..
  • Gas heater with no gas smell: turn the gas control knob to its off position..
  • Close the cold water inlet valve on the pipe at the top of the tank by turning the handle across the pipe..
  • Keep children and pets out of the area, and move anything valuable off the floor..
  • Do not touch the hot water coming from a discharge pipe or valve. It can scald..

Leak source one: the relief valve or its drain pipe

.

The temperature and pressure relief valve sits on the side or top of the tank, with a pipe running down toward the floor. Water dripping from the end of that pipe means the valve is opening. It may be reacting to real overpressure, or the valve itself may be worn.

Never cap, plug or block that pipe. The valve is the tank's safety release, and sealing it removes the protection. If it drips every time the heater runs, call a plumber. A valve that leaks constantly or sprays hot water needs to be replaced, and repeated pressure spikes can point to a problem elsewhere in the system.

Leak source two: the drain valve at the bottom

.

Near the base of the tank is a drain valve that looks like an outdoor spigot. Its job is to let you flush sediment. It can seep from the stem or from the threads where it enters the tank, especially if it has not been touched in years.

With the heater shut off and the tank cool, you can check that a cap is on the outlet and that the handle is fully closed. Tighten gently by hand. If it still drips, replacing the valve usually means draining some of the tank, which is a job for a plumber. A small seep from this valve is a repair, not a verdict on the tank.

Leak source three: the tank has failed

.

If water seeps from the seam at the base, from the jacket, or from beneath the insulation, and none of the valves or fittings is wet, the inner steel tank has most likely corroded through. Corrosion cannot be patched. The leak will grow, and a heater in this condition can fail without warning.

Mineral sediment inside the tank, an old unit and a tank that rumbles or pops are common companions of this failure, though none of them proves it. A plumber can confirm by tracing the water to its origin. When the tank is the source, the answer is water heater replacement, and the sooner it is planned, the less damage the leak does.

A quick way to narrow it down

.

With the water and power or gas off, dry the area and check these spots in order.

  • Pipe connections on top of the tank, where the hot and cold lines enter.
  • Relief valve and its discharge pipe on the side.
  • Drain valve near the bottom.
  • Gas line connection or burner compartment on a gas unit, by smell only and not by touch.
  • The pan or floor under the tank, in case water is running in from another source such as a nearby pipe or appliance.

Repair or replace: the questions that decide it

.

Age matters, but condition matters more. A relief valve, a drain valve or a loose fitting is a repair on a heater that is otherwise sound. A leaking tank is not repairable, and neither is a unit that has needed several repairs in a short span.

Ask whether the unit has been rumbling, producing rusty water, or struggling to keep up. Those signs plus a wet base lean toward replacement. If the heater is in good shape and the leak is at a fitting, repair is the sensible path.

A replacement is also the time to think about size, fuel type and whether a tankless model suits the household. Your plumber can walk through those choices and give a clear price before work starts.

American Canyon note: earthquake bracing and your heater

.

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters, and existing residential ones, to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion, following the California Plumbing Code. It covers standard heaters up to 120 gallons, and a preengineered kit is available. The August 24, 2014 South Napa earthquake had its epicenter about 3.7 miles northwest of American Canyon, so a heater that tips or shifts here is not a theoretical problem.

If your heater is leaking, look at the straps while you are there. A loose or missing strap is worth fixing during the same visit, because a tank that moves can break its own pipes and gas line.

What changes the cost of a leaking water heater visit

.

A valve or fitting repair is usually a short job. The cost depends on the part, how accessible it is, and whether the tank must be drained. Sediment-heavy tanks and corroded fittings can take longer.

Replacement pricing depends on tank size, fuel type, venting, and what is needed to meet current code, including the bracing rule above. Water damage nearby adds work of its own.
