Active problem

Water is on the floor and customers are inside

.

A flooded floor is a safety problem before it is a plumbing problem. Slippery tile, electrical equipment and food areas all raise the stakes, so the first minutes are about protecting people and stopping the source.

  1. Move customers and staff away from the wet area and block it off..
  2. Shut off the water at the building's main valve or the fixture's stop valve if you can reach it safely..
  3. Turn off power to equipment standing in water only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water..
  4. Photograph the damage for your insurer before anything is moved or cleaned up..

Commercial drain service, floor drain overflow and lift pumps

.

Commercial drains carry more than a house does. Floor drains in kitchens, back rooms and mechanical spaces take wash water, debris and sometimes grease. When one overflows, the cause is usually a blockage further down the line, and clearing only the visible part of the drain brings the backup back in days.

We clear the line with a cable or a jetting machine, depending on what is in it, and look at the pipe afterward when the problem keeps repeating. See drain cleaning for how that works, and hydro jetting for heavy buildup that a cable leaves behind.

Buildings with a basement, a sump or a lift pump have an extra failure point. If the pump fails or its float sticks, wastewater has nowhere to go, so we check the pump, the check valve and the discharge line together.

Grease trap cleaning

.

A grease trap catches fats and food solids before they reach the sewer line. Once it fills, the grease passes through and hardens in downstream pipes, which is why a restaurant can have a sudden line backup right after a busy weekend.

Cleaning means pumping out the contents, scraping the walls and checking the baffles and lids. How often depends on how much cooking you do, so a schedule that suits a bakery will not suit a fryer-heavy kitchen. We can set a routine with you, and the City of American Canyon can tell you what it expects for your business.

Backflow testing is planned service, so ask the city first

.

A backflow assembly sits on a water line to keep used or contaminated water from flowing backward into the clean supply. It is a mechanical device with seals and springs, so it needs to be tested to confirm it still works.

The facts we have do not include a specific American Canyon rule, so we state none here. Ask the City of American Canyon water department about its backflow program requirements, including who must test and how reports are filed. Once you know what applies, call us to schedule the testing and any repair of a failed assembly.

Storm drains on your property or in your lot

.

Only rain belongs in a storm drain here. Those drains flow untreated to local creeks, then the Napa River and San Francisco Bay, so wash water, grease and debris must not go down them. Leaves in gutters or inlets also violate Municipal Code 14.28. For a business with a lot, that means pressure-washing runoff and kitchen mats cannot simply go to the nearest inlet.

Public Works vacuums storm drain inlets before the wet season, but private catch basins and area drains on your lot are your concern. We clean and clear those so water leaves your parking area instead of pooling at the door. If you are unsure which drain is yours, call the city's Environmental Services at (707) 647-5325 before work.

What drives the price of commercial work

.

The size of the building and the hour of the call both change the bill.

Type of job

A drain clearing, a pump replacement and a leak hunt each take different labor and equipment.

Building access

Roof access, locked mechanical rooms and finished ceilings add time.

After-hours timing

Urgent work at night or on a weekend is priced differently from a scheduled visit.

Parts and equipment

Pumps, valves and backflow parts vary by size and type.

Follow-up work

Cameras, repeat visits or repairs found during the job are quoted separately before they begin.

Business insurance policies differ. Many cover sudden and accidental water damage but not slow leaks or worn-out parts, so ask your insurer, and photograph the damage before cleanup.
